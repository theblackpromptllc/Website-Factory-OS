- Durham County is approximately 31.8% Black/African American, ~105,711 residents — well above the North Carolina state average of ~20%. [Source 2]

ANALYSIS — why this matters through the lens:
Durham is a genuinely strong market for this business specifically because the Black population share is roughly 1.5x the state average. This is not a niche-within-a-niche; it is a large, established, multi-generational Black community (Durham's "Black Wall Street" / Hayti history is part of the city's identity). The customer base for specialized natural hair care is dense and local, not something the salon has to import or convert.

FACT — Service landscape and price signals confirmed in-market (Durham):
- Silk press is an actively offered and searched service; one established salon (Amaka's) lists Silk Press from $55+, Silk Press with treatment from $65+. [Source 3, 4]
- Loc retwist is a high-frequency, recurring-revenue service. Market-range pricing seen across Durham providers: short loc retwist roughly $75–$100+, with explicit "additional fee for the amount of locs and length." [Source 5]
- Knotless/box braids in Durham run a market range of roughly $150–$330, 7–11 hours, varying by size and length. [Source 6]
- Faux locs with hair included start around $145+ at one named loc salon (CanaryCoiffure). [Source 7]

ANALYSIS — market structure:
The Durham textured-hair market is mature and segmented into recognizable lanes:
- Loc specialists / locticians (locs as a craft and identity)
- Braiders / protective-style shops (often African-braiding heritage shops)
- Silk-press + healthy-natural-hair salons
- Extension / sew-in boutiques
Most established players are 20+ years old (Amaka's, Taji, Natural Roots by Jey), which means the barrier to entry is reputation and trust, not service availability. A new entrant competes on a clear point of view and client experience, not on "being the only one who can do this."

4. CUSTOMER PERSONAS (ANALYSIS — built from a Black standpoint)
================================================================

These are inferred personas (analysis), grounded in the verified market above. The cultural context is named in each, not assumed.

PERSONA A — "The Loc Journey Keeper"
- Black woman, ~25–45, locs are part of her identity, not just a style. Returns every 4–8 weeks for retwist/maintenance.
- Wants: a loctician who respects loc health, won't over-tighten (thinning/traction concern), and is consistent and on-time.
- Cultural read: locs carry cultural and personal meaning; she wants a stylist who gets that, not one who treats locs as "dreads." Trust is built over years; switching salons feels risky.
- Hook for Rooted: scalp/root health focus + reliable scheduling + respect for the loc journey.

PERSONA B — "The Silk-Press, Heat-Damage-Wary Professional"
- Black woman, ~28–50, natural hair, wants a polished silk press for work/events but is afraid of heat damage and reversion done wrong.
- Wants: proven heat-safe technique, deep conditioning, a stylist who can give sleek results without sacrificing her curl pattern long-term.
- Cultural read: she has likely been burned (literally or by heat damage) before; "I trust them with my hair" is the entire decision. Word of mouth in her network is decisive.
- Hook for Rooted: heat-protectant/healthy-silk-press messaging + treatment bundling.

PERSONA C — "The Protective-Style Planner"
- Black woman, ~22–40, books knotless braids/protective styles seasonally to protect length and save daily styling time.
- Wants: clean parts, low-tension (no edges damage), durable styles, transparent pricing, realistic time estimates.
- Cultural read: very price- and time-aware (braids are a multi-hour, $150–$330 commitment); she shares stylist recommendations actively and screenshots portfolios.
- Hook for Rooted: clear pricing tiers, portfolio gallery, edge/tension care, on-time promise.

PERSONA D — "The New-to-Durham Searcher"
- Black woman who recently moved to Durham (RTP draws transplants for jobs/school) and urgently needs a trusted stylist for HER hair, with no local network yet.
- Wants: to quickly verify "this place gets type 4 hair and locs," via reviews, real photos of textured hair like hers, and easy online booking.
- Cultural read: she relies on #BlackLocticians, StyleSeat/Booksy, IG, and Black-Durham word-of-mouth groups; a generic salon site with only straight-hair photos signals "not for us" and she bounces.
- Hook for Rooted: SEO + a website with authentic textured-hair photos, reviews, and instant booking. This persona is the single biggest argument for a strong web presence.

DEFAULT-CHECK across personas: none of these default to a "general consumer." The deciding factor across all four is trust that the stylist specializes in HER texture/locs — not price alone, not proximity alone. A market read that ignored that would mis-prioritize the website.

6. OPPORTUNITY GAPS (ANALYSIS)
================================================================

Gaps below are interpretation, grounded in the verified landscape. The lens question driving each: where is this community underserved by EXPERIENCE even though it is well-served by service availability?

GAP 1 — One trusted studio for the whole natural-hair journey.
The market is split: purists do locs-only-no-extensions (Natural Roots), braiders do braids, boutiques do sew-ins, spas do silk press. A client whose needs span locs + the occasional silk press + protective styles + scalp care must juggle multiple providers. A single credible studio doing all of it WELL — without making her feel she has to compromise — is differentiated. (Counter-note: depth-vs-breadth is a real tradeoff; Rooted must prove specialist-level skill in each, not look like a generalist — see Gap 5.)

GAP 2 — Scalp and root health as a named specialty.
"Scalp care" and "deep conditioning treatments" are in Rooted's service list and echoed in its name ("Rooted"), but most competitors lead with styles (locs, braids, silk press), not scalp/root HEALTH. Owning "healthy scalp + healthy roots" — treatments, low-tension styling, traction-alopecia awareness, loc-health — is an under-claimed, on-brand position with real cultural resonance (edges/tension and scalp health are widely discussed concerns in this community).

GAP 3 — Heat-damage-safe silk press, stated explicitly.
Persona B's core fear is heat damage. Competitors offer silk press; few visibly promise and explain a heat-safe, curl-protecting method. Saying it plainly on the site is a low-cost, high-trust differentiator.

GAP 4 — Modern booking + transparent pricing + real portfolio.
Aggregators win search partly because salon sites under-deliver on booking, clear pricing tiers, and authentic textured-hair galleries. Braids/protective-style clients especially want transparent price/time up front (it's a $150–$330, multi-hour commitment). A site with instant booking, honest pricing tiers, and a real portfolio of type 3–4 hair and locs directly serves Persona C and Persona D.

GAP 5 — Specialist signaling against generalists.
Generalist salons that "also" do natural hair (and even do relaxers/silkouts) leave room for a studio that signals unambiguous specialization in textured/natural hair and locs. For a heat-damage-wary or loc-committed client, "we specialize in YOUR hair, only" is a stronger promise than "we do everything."

GAP 6 — The New-to-Durham / RTP transplant.
RTP/universities continuously bring in Black women with no local stylist network. They search online and decide fast based on reviews, real photos, and easy booking. Most established competitors rely on decades of word-of-mouth and have weaker web capture. A modern, well-optimized, culturally authentic website is Rooted's clearest competitive wedge for this continuously-renewing audience.

DEFAULT-CHECK on the gaps: none of these gaps is "the community lacks salons" — they are well served on service availability and are well represented (Black-owned, natural-hair-centered) in their own market. The real gaps are experience, trust-signaling, scalp/root-health positioning, and web/booking capture. Naming that honestly is more useful than a false "underserved market" claim.
