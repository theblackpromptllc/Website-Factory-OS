---

## 1. BUSINESS SUMMARY

- **Business:** Bright Minds Homeschool Tutoring
- **Category:** Supplemental education / homeschool instructional support
- **Model:** One-on-one + small group tutoring, delivered to homeschool families
- **Age focus:** Children 5–10 (early elementary / foundational years: phonics, early reading, handwriting, number sense, early math fluency)
- **Geography:** North Carolina (statewide; relevant to NC's high homeschool density and NC-3 compliance environment)
- **Buyer vs. user split:** Parent is the buyer/decision-maker and primary contact; child is the end user — website must speak to the parent, not the child
- **Service nature:** High-trust, relationship-driven, recurring (not transactional) — implies lead-capture + consultation flow over instant checkout

---

## 2. MARKET OVERVIEW

- **Market category:** Homeschool support services — a sub-segment of the broader tutoring market, distinct from test-prep (Sylvan/Kumon) and distinct from full-curriculum providers
- **Demand drivers:** NC has one of the largest homeschool populations in the U.S.; sustained post-2020 growth; parents teaching outside their subject confidence (esp. reading instruction/phonics and early math)
- **Buyer cycles:** Tied to academic calendar — peak intent **July–September** (year start) and **January** (mid-year correction); summer remediation is a secondary window
- **Delivery modes in market:** In-person (home/library/co-op), local learning pods, and online 1:1 — website must clarify which modes are offered and service radius
- **Price sensitivity:** Moderate-to-high; homeschool families are budget-conscious and value-driven, but will pay for measurable progress and credentialed/specialized instruction (e.g., Orton-Gillingham, dyslexia-aware)
- **Trust currency:** Reviews, parent testimonials, tutor credentials, and "she gets my kid" relational proof outweigh polish

## 4. CUSTOMER PERSONAS

### PRIMARY — "The Overwhelmed Homeschool Mom"
- **Profile:** Mother, 30–45, homeschooling 1–3 kids, often the sole educator at home
- **Context:** Confident in some subjects, hitting a wall in others (commonly reading/phonics or math fluency)
- **Pain points:** Doubt about doing it "right," fear of a child falling behind, decision fatigue, isolation, time scarcity
- **Desires:** A trusted expert partner, visible progress, reassurance she's not failing her child, one less subject to carry
- **Fears:** "Am I damaging my child's future?", judgment from family/school officials, wasting money on something that doesn't work
- **Objections:** Cost, "can I just do it myself?", trust in a stranger with my child, scheduling around our flexible day
- **Trigger moments:** Child crying over lessons; a reading/math plateau; an evaluation flagging a gap; burnout

### SECONDARY — "The Working/Hybrid Homeschool Parent"
- **Profile:** Dual-income or single working parent using a hybrid model (homeschool + outsourced instruction); 32–48
- **Context:** Limited daytime availability; outsources subjects they can't personally cover
- **Pain points:** No time to teach core subjects, guilt over outsourcing, coordination overhead
- **Desires:** Reliable, scheduled, done-for-me instruction; progress reporting they can review on their own time
- **Fears:** Losing control/visibility of child's progress; inconsistent tutors
- **Objections:** Scheduling rigidity, price-per-hour, online vs. in-person fit
- **Trigger moments:** New school year planning; a job/schedule change; falling behind on a subject they outsourced

## 6. OPPORTUNITY GAPS

- **Specialist positioning gap:** No major brand owns "early-elementary (ages 5–10) homeschool reading & math specialist" — claimable niche
- **Trust/proof gap:** Independents lack systematized social proof; a site leading with parent testimonials, tutor credentials, and visible progress tracking out-positions solo competitors
- **Reassurance gap:** Parents search from a place of fear/self-doubt — content that validates the parent ("you're not behind, you're getting help") is an underserved emotional entry point
- **Onboarding-friction gap:** Competitors push to enrollment/checkout; whitespace exists for a low-commitment **free assessment / consultation** offer matched to the high-trust buying cycle
- **Local NC gap:** National platforms are generic; NC-specific pages (homeschool law/NC-3 awareness, NC city service areas, co-op partnerships) capture local search the big players ignore
- **Scheduling-fit gap:** Homeschool days are flexible/non-standard — explicit "we work around your homeschool schedule" messaging differentiates from rigid center-based franchises
- **Progress-visibility gap:** Secondary persona (working parents) is underserved by competitors lacking reporting — async progress updates are a differentiator for design/UX downstream
